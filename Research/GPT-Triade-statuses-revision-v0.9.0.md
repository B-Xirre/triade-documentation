# Triade Status and Effects System

In game design, **status effects** are temporary or persistent conditions that modify a character’s attributes or abilities.  They include buffs (positive effects) and debuffs (negative effects) and are central to combat and tactics.  For Triade, character statuses (e.g. *bleeding*, *stunned*, *poisoned*) and environmental states (e.g. *burning floor*, *electrified surface*, *slippery ground*) must be defined as part of the core mechanics.  We propose a unified data model where each status effect has a name, category (Buff/Debuff/Hazard), applied target (character or terrain), and properties such as magnitude, duration type, and stack behavior.  Conceptually, statuses bundle together *enter*, *ongoing*, and *exit* effects, as in RPG systems.  For example, a **Bleeding** status (debuff) might enter when a character takes critical injury, then cause ongoing hit-point loss each turn until healed, and exit when a bandage or medkit is used.  Similarly, a **Frozen** status could immobilize a character (unable to move) until warmed by a fire or time passage. Each status’s lifecycle is governed by entry conditions (when it is applied) and exit conditions (when it is removed).  These can be time-based (lasting N turns) or conditional (until specific healing), as is standard in games. 

We prepare a **Status Effects Table** outlining key Triade statuses, their categories, effects, durations, and mitigations.  This table forms the basis of the game’s status data model:

| **Status**    | **Category**        | **Effect**                                | **Duration**                    | **Mitigation**                           |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Bleeding**  | Debuff (Physical)   | Lose HP each turn (damage over time)       | Until healed / bandaged         | Bandages/Medkit stops bleed (cures DoT)    |
| **Burning**   | Debuff (Fire/Hazard)| Take fire damage each turn                | Until extinguished or wears off | Water or fireproof armor extinguishes fire |
| **Poisoned**  | Debuff (Toxic)      | Take poison damage over time             | 5–10 turns or until cured       | Antidote item removes poison  |
| **Stunned**   | Debuff             | Skip next action(s); cannot act          | 1–2 turns                       | Auto-recovers next turn; special skills    |
| **Chilled**   | Debuff (Cold)      | Slowed movement and fire vulnerability   | 3–5 turns                       | Warmth (campfire) or anti-freeze potion   |
| **Electrified**| Debuff (Electric)  | Damage over time and chance to stun       | 3 turns or until grounded       | Rubber boots/insulator removes effect     |
| **Buffed**    | Buff               | +Damage, +Defense, or +Speed              | 5–10 turns                      | Naturally expires; dispel spell          |
| **Shielded**  | Buff               | Temporary extra hit points or absorb damage | N turns or points             | Breaks when capacity used; shield potion   |
| **Blinded**   | Debuff             | -Hit chance, area attacks only            | 2 turns or until cleansed       | Blindfold removal or “see eye” ability    |
| **Prone**     | Debuff (Position)   | Reduced defense; must spend turn to stand | Until recovery                  | Stand-up action removes it               |
| **Shocked Floor** | Hazard (Env)   | Turns floor into electrified surface      | Persistent until powered down   | Turn off generator or use insulation      |
| **On Fire Floor** | Hazard (Env)   | Area causes burning status on occupants   | Persistent (until put out)      | Water or extinguisher removes hazard      |
| **Radiation** | Debuff (Elemental)  | Continuous damage and radiation sickness  | Persistent or until antidote    | Anti-radiation serum                       |

These statuses illustrate **triade-specific needs**: some (like *Shielded* or *Buffed*) are **temporary positive effects**, while others (*Bleeding*, *Poisoned*, *Burning*, *Electrified*) are **ongoing damage-over-time** debuffs linked to the damage/health system.  Environmental hazards (e.g. electrified floor) are modeled similarly but attached to map tiles rather than characters. Each entry would correspond to a row in Triade’s Effects tables, with fields such as *magnitude*, *duration*, *stack limit*, and *source*.  

In practice, the **data model** for statuses should record: status ID/name, category (buff/debuff/hazard), affected attributes (e.g. HP, speed), start time, remaining duration or permanent flag, and conditions for auto-removal.  We align this with Triade’s existing **TemporaryEffects** and **PersistentEffects** tables (as noted in the design docs).  For example, a temporary effect entry might store “Bleeding, duration 5 seconds, damage=2 per sec”, while a persistent effect entry might be “Shielded, persists until value=0”.  Exit conditions (when the status ends) trigger effects like healing or stat restoration.  By structuring statuses this way, we can efficiently allocate, update, and expire effects at runtime.

**Mitigation** of statuses is handled via game mechanics.  Typically, items or abilities specifically cure or reduce statuses: for instance, in many games (e.g. Final Fantasy) an *Antidote* cures Poison, *Esuna* cures most debuffs, and a *Remedy* removes multiple.  Triade can follow suit: a medkit might remove Bleeding, a fire extinguisher removes Burning on surfaces, insulation grants immunity to Electrified, etc.  We also allow natural recovery for transient statuses (e.g. *Stun* ends after a turn).  Critical or “persistent” statuses (like *Radiation*) might require rare cures or longer processes.  Finally, combat tactics can invoke statuses as intermediate triggers: for instance, a *Dazed* status (light debuff) could give enemies advantage against that target until it’s “invoked” by some action. 

In summary, we recommend a **comprehensive status table** with entries for all game-affecting states, linked to Triade’s damage/health system. Each status would be stored with metadata (type, duration, magnitude) and connected to Triade’s effect tables for application and removal.  This ensures each status is properly applied (on hit/event), updated (per turn or time), and mitigated (by items or conditions), consistent with standard RPG design.  

## Procedural Dungeon Generation Pipeline

For dungeon generation, we adopt a **constraint-based procedural approach** using *Wave Function Collapse* (WFC), also known as *Model Synthesis*.  WFC treats map generation as a constraint satisfaction problem: each cell in the map is a “superposition” of possible tile states, which is then **collapsed** into one consistent state. In practice, Triade designers provide a set of prototype tile templates (e.g. room modules, corridor sections, environmental tiles), and the algorithm randomly assigns and propagates compatible tiles until the space is filled.  As explained in WFC design, the algorithm repeatedly selects the cell with the lowest entropy (fewest options), fixes it to one tile, and then enforces adjacency rules on its neighbors. This “collapse and propagate” process continues until the dungeon layout is complete. Critically, WFC can operate on a **3D grid** just as well as 2D, enabling truly multi-level designs (rooms with two-floor verticality or dungeons with multiple stacked levels).  

**Key steps in the dungeon pipeline:**

- **1. Layout Partitioning:** We first divide the dungeon volume into zones or rooms.  A practical method is **Binary Space Partitioning (BSP)**: recursively split the space into random rectangles (or cuboids). This ensures connectivity and traditionally rectangular rooms.  BSP defines where each room/corridor block lies.

- **2. WFC Room Generation:** Within each BSP-defined block, run a WFC instance.  Treat the block as a 3D grid of cells.  The input to WFC is a set of **tile prototypes** (from artists or AI) that fit together; for example, a small room layout or tile pattern.  The algorithm generates a **unique room interior** by collating these patterns.  Because each block uses its own WFC instance, rooms remain distinct and varied even with a rectangular outline.  As one study notes: *“using BSP… we can keep the iconic rectangular dungeon designs… while also making each dungeon room unique and distinct from the rest”*. 

- **3. Connectivity and Proofing:** After rooms are generated, we ensure they connect.  We use BSP’s backtracking or corridor-carving to link rooms into a traversable map.  If WFC fails to seal a room properly, additional corridors can be programmatically added (as in [26†L714-L723]).  Automated checks verify there are no isolated sections.  Because WFC is stochastic, the pipeline may generate multiple candidates and select those that satisfy gameplay metrics (size, connectivity, difficulty).  For example, a convolutional neural network or heuristic can evaluate a level’s difficulty or spacing.

- **4. Agentic and Human Authoring:** At each stage, we incorporate agentic (AI-assisted) and manual steps.  For instance, an AI might suggest placements of key objects (treasures, enemies) or fine-tune layouts, while a human designer reviews and edits critical rooms. This human-in-the-loop approach ensures both scale and coherence.  Designers can sketch high-level shapes, which the system fleshes out (or vice versa), and then manually refine.  As one procedural framework suggests, keeping designers central in the pipeline yields better results. The final maps may undergo playtesting and human polish, especially to place narrative elements or fix subtle flow issues.

This pipeline blends **WFC/model synthesis with classic techniques**. BSP provides structure and efficiency (avoiding WFC’s combinatorial explosion on a single huge grid), while WFC injects variety. Indeed, combining both “leverages the benefits of both algorithms”: BSP maintains rectangular rooms and easy linking, and WFC gives each room a pseudo-human aesthetic with rich detail.  The result is a *highly flexible dungeon generator*, capable of producing many unique layouts quickly.  Because we run WFC in separate chunks (rooms), the process can be parallelized to speed up generation.  

### Tile Generation Module

We also establish a **separate tile-generation pipeline** for creating the building blocks (tiles) that WFC uses.  This pipeline goes from concept to useable tile assets:

- **Concept and Design:** Artists define tile **themes** and prototype shapes.  For example, designers may draw 2D tile sprites or 3D block models for floor segments, walls, doors, obstacles, etc.  Each tile includes metadata about how it can connect (e.g. “wall tile” vs “door tile”).  Connectivity constraints can be encoded (such as matching edge types or colors) to guide WFC placement.

- **Rule Definition:** Each tile type is given adjacency rules. For instance, edge “plugs” or colors ensure doors line up with corridors, walls with walls, etc.  In effect, the tile set becomes a CSP (constraint satisfaction problem) as described by Dykeman.  Triade’s tiles must also incorporate game mechanics: e.g. a tile with a gun rack (combat object), or an elevator, or a trap trigger.  These special object tiles are marked so the generator can place objects accordingly.

- **Tile Synthesis (Bulk Generation):** Using WFC or similar, we can algorithmically generate variations of tiles or small tile maps.  For example, one can run WFC on a set of base images to produce many floor/wall textures that fit together.  A tool like WaveFunctionCollapse can synthesize new patterns from samples.  For 3D tiles (e.g. modular wall pieces), designers ensure vertex/edge matching to avoid gaps.  Essentially, WFC on the **tile level** can create diverse room layouts or terrain from a small input set, with local adjacency enforced.  All generated tiles are then **proofed**: automated checks ensure seams align and that each tile set can fully tile a region without conflicts (backtracking checks may be applied if needed).  Any failed or invalid tiles are discarded or corrected by the AI or an artist.

- **Iteration and Polish:** Generated tiles are reviewed by artists (human authoring). Designers may iteratively adjust colors, add detail, or modify tile shapes.  The pipeline tracks each tile’s origin and rule set in the data model, so changes propagate appropriately. Finally, tiles are exported with game-ready formats (meshes, textures, collision data, nav meshes) and metadata (which status or object they represent, if any). 

This separation ensures that **tile assets** are robust before being assembled into full dungeons.  By combining procedural techniques with human design, Triade obtains both high variability (via WFC synthesis) and artistic quality.

### Verticality and Multi-Level Dungeons

Triade’s design explicitly allows **vertical dungeons**.  By using WFC on a three-dimensional grid, rooms can span multiple floors.  We simply parameterize the dungeon height.  For example, a two-story room is a 2×1×1 block (X×Y×Z) and WFC treats the Z dimension like another adjacency axis.  This lets us create features such as staircases, balconies, or rooms with high ceilings.  When partitioning (BSP), we can specify how many vertical layers a section has.  The pipeline then collapses tiles in 3D: floor tiles in one layer propagate constraints to the layer above/below.  Corridors and elevators connect levels, and the algorithm ensures consistency (e.g. a wall segment in one layer matches a wall above it).  In effect, we **stack WFC-generated floors** and link them.  This approach generalizes naturally: as one developer noted, “the dimensionality of the matrix is going to match the dimensionality of our data”, which in Triade’s case can be 3D.

### Integration with Triade Mechanics

Finally, the generation must respect Triade’s locked-in mechanics. This means **game objects and environmental statuses** are incorporated into generation rules. For instance:

- **Combat Objects:** Tiles that contain cover, weapons, or interactable consoles must be placed logically (e.g. cover behind walls, ammo near spawn).  Our tile prototypes will include object markers, and WFC constraints ensure they only appear in valid contexts (e.g. a gun rack on a wall tile, not in open air).

- **Surface Statuses:** Some tiles have special surfaces (e.g. electrically charged floors, slippery ice, hazardous gas).  These are treated like status effects on the environment. The generation rules should allow these tiles only in appropriate rooms (for example, a “power room” may contain an Electrified Floor tile).  The status system ties into this: standing on an electrified tile would apply the **Electrified** status to a character.

- **Rule Preservation:** Since Triade has other mechanics (like cover blocking line-of-fire, area effects), the tile generation rulesets incorporate these. For example, if a room is marked as “combat arena”, the BSP partition might force certain tile patterns (more open space, multiple cover options). 

By planning tiles and rules around Triade’s damage/health and combat design, generated dungeons seamlessly support gameplay. The final pipeline – from procedural layout to assembled, playable map – yields rich, multi-level dungeons with full status-effect interactions, as required.
